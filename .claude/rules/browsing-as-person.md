# Web Browsing: acting as a person

See also: `web-browsing.md` (tools, overlays, tabs), `web-images.md` (page images), `camoufox.md` (the browser container).

## Browsing as a person

Logins are handed off, never typed by the model: the person logs in on a live view of a tab in their own browser context and the agent inherits the session. `.scratch/web-identity/spec.md` is the design; what follows is what the code cannot say for itself.

**A person is their account, resolved and never claimed.** Browsing as a person reads the account the turn's principal carries (`PersonResolution.Of`, `Domain/Tools/Web`): `Principal.Account.UserId` is the person and keys their browser context, live-only logins, sealed record and logins page. The account is the verification — the chat client's from the proxy's session check, Telegram's from its linked numeric id, a voice's from its recognised profile (`.claude/rules/chat-hub.md`, `.claude/rules/telegram.md`, `.claude/rules/voice.md`) — so there is no list of verified channels, and every channel of one account reaches the same context and logins. A channel's own name for someone (a Telegram username, a display name) is never looked up. A principal with no account is nobody, and `asMe`, `web_login` and `web_logins` refuse it by name: the voice guest is told their voice was not recognised; anything else — a fire no delegation stands behind, a call signed for nobody (a context with no principal names nobody, whatever `UserId` it carries), a principal stamped before accounts — that nobody vouched for an account. Nothing falls back to the anonymous context or to another person. A record sealed under a person's name from before accounts is the household migration's to re-seal (`HouseholdMigration/Steps/RememberedLogins.cs`), never read as a fallback.

**A principal is only as true as the channel that stamped it.** A turn's `ConversationContext.Principal` (`Domain/DTOs/Channel/Principal.cs`) is who it acts for: a person's own message is vouched for by its channel (`DeliveryTargetResolver.BuildConversationContext`), on a server by the call token (`CallTurn`), and a context without one is nobody. A schedule's, watch's or login wake's fire acts for the person whose turn last wrote the object, on the delegation the agent signed for that write and checks as the fire arrives (`.claude/rules/delegations.md`, "Deferred turns"), never on a schedule JSON's `userId` or a server-stamped principal; a download's fire, or any whose delegation does not hold, is nobody. A login wake's delegation is the one `web_login`'s call carried, kept on the handoff (`LiveHandoff.Delegation`) for Done's wake and a voice login's link alike. A fire's `Origin.ChannelId` is only where it delivers and its `UserId` only what its author wrote, so neither is read (a `deliverTo: ["telegram"]` schedule naming a Telegram username once resolved as that person).

**The person is asked before the agent acts as them on a site.** `asMe` is a flag the model sets per call, so a page's injected text can ask for it.
- Agent-side, `ToolApprovalChatClient` puts any call carrying `asMe: true` to the person, whatever the whitelist or a remembered tool says, with reason code `as_person` (`ExecScreenCodes.AsPerson`). Approve-and-remember covers that tool on that site (`AsPersonSubject`: the tool plus `RegistrableDomain.Of` the url's host) for the rest of the conversation, never the tool itself, kept apart from the tool-level set the exec bridge reads. A call with no parseable url is asked every time.
- A schedule's, watch's or download's fire is not asked — its approvals go where nobody is waiting — and runs as the whitelist says, as the person its delegation names. A login wake is asked like the person's own turn. Refs carry no flag, so `web_action` on an `asMe` tab is not asked again.
- **Server-side, `asMe` reaches only a site the person holds a login for** (`AsMeGate` over `ILoginSites`, which `PersonLogins.HasLoginAsync` answers): any other is refused before anything opens, pointing at `web_login` on the person's own turn and at a report on a fire. That bounds a fire, which nothing asks, and what an approved site's tab can be talked into.
- Still open: a schedule written during the person's own turn runs as them later without asking, so injected text could set one up; the approval of the schedule's write is the gate there.

**Contexts, not fingerprints, separate people.** The browser holds the anonymous context (every browse without `asMe`, carrying nobody's cookies) and one person context per person, made on first use over a connection (`PlaywrightWebBrowser.ContextForAsync`) and restored from remembered state. A tab belongs to its context for life (`BrowserTab.Context`); reuse-before-open and the composed snapshot's address match only within it, and a popup joins its opener's. Refs need no flag — `web_action`, `view_image`, `web_snapshot` and `back` act as the person exactly when the ref's tab is theirs, which is how a worker on its parent's session reaches the parent's `asMe` tabs. The envelope names `asMe`. A reconnect closes every context; person contexts come back lazily, restored.

**The handoff tab is outside every pool.** `web_login` opens its url through `IHandoffBrowser` (`PlaywrightHandoffTabs`): a page in the person's context no browse session admits, so no ref, browse or action reaches it while the person types.
- Refused on any turn with an `OriginKind` (schedule, watch, download, the handoff's own turns) and withheld from workers agent-side (`AgentSpec.WithheldTools`, with `web_logins`), because a worker's calls carry its parent's context.
- Who may be handed a link is the Domain tools' (`WebLoginTool`, `WebLoginsTool`, over `ILoginHandoffs`/`ILoginsPages`); the tab, link and view are the server's (`LoginHandoffs`, `LoginsPages`); `IHandoffBrowser`, `IPersonContexts` and `IPersonStateStore` live in Infrastructure, since no Domain code consumes them.
- The link (`OneTimeLinks`, Redis, single use) is `Handoff:PublicBaseUrl` + `/browse/<household>/handoff/<token>` (`BrowsePagePath`, the household of the account the call acts for). Its route — Caddy, the agent's relay, the household's tunnel, the pages listener (8081), which serves nothing else — is `.claude/rules/tunnel-forwarding.md` ("Browse pages") and `.claude/rules/mcp-hosting.md`.
- **The link's GET only shows the page**; previewers and prefetch issue GETs, so the page's own `POST …/claim` spends the token atomically and binds an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie (`Secure` behind Caddy's `X-Forwarded-Proto`) on `Path=/browse/<household>` — no other household's server is sent it — which every later request answers to; the page makes every request under its own household's path.
- A claim for a tab this process no longer holds refuses (a ticket outlives a restart, its tab does not). A Done whose tab the browser lost answers 410 and wakes nobody.
- The handoff tab's dialogs are dismissed (the screencast never shows one). Its visited hosts are every main-frame navigation request, redirect hops included, since a single sign-on's intermediate pages set cookies of their own.
- `LoginHandoffs` keeps live handoffs in memory with one `TimeProvider` timer: ten minutes to claim, thirty for the view (`Handoff:TicketMinutes`, `Handoff:ViewMinutes`); either running out closes the tab and emits nothing.
- **A handoff's hold on the person's logins is one object**: `PersonLogins.BeginHandoffAsync` hands out a `HandoffLease` before the tab opens, the `LiveHandoff` carries it, and every way out — a failed mint or open, Done, Done on a lost tab, expiry — settles it with `CompleteAsync` or `AbandonAsync`. The first settle wins, later ones are no-ops, an unsettled lease abandons on dispose, and "a handoff is open" means the person holds an unsettled lease; there is no counter to pair by hand (two earlier bugs).

**The live view is a screencast, drawn at the view's own size.**
- Frames are `page.Screencast` JPEGs sent as binary WebSocket messages, newest only — a frame arriving mid-send is dropped. The Camoufox image patches playwright-core not to wait for a per-frame client ack (1.63's server waits for one) the .NET client never sends, which froze the view on its first frame (`DockerCompose/camoufox/patch-screencast-ack.js`); test fixtures reuse an existing `camoufox:latest`, so rebuild it after the patch changes.
- **The view says its size** (`HandoffInput.Resize`, CSS pixels of its picture box, sent on connect and when its width changes — never for a keyboard that only shortens it, or the site reflows under the field). The tab and every popup it opens are drawn at it (clamped 320–1920), so a phone gets the site's phone layout; the screencast restarts at that shape, up to twice the CSS size and 1600 px on the long side. With no size asked the tab keeps 1920x1080.
- **Camoufox's screencast draws a page as if it were never scrolled** while clicks land where it is, so a scrolled page's frames are viewport screenshots (`HandoffStream.ShowAsync`, caret left as it is so no style is added to the site), the screencast frame only signalling a change; disabling APZ did not help. A site's own scrolling box counts too (a capturing `scroll` listener the page script leaves, sticky for the page's life). The screencast sends ~20 frames a second regardless, so shots are held to five a second (`_shotInterval`).
- **One view at a time**: a newer stream of a handoff ends the older before it casts, so a reconnecting phone is never frozen by the socket it left behind.
- Every 250 ms the page on top reports whether it is scrolled and where its text fields are (`FieldsScript`: main frame only, rect and keyboard kind, never a value) to the view as a `fields` message. Input comes back as JSON at fractions of the frame and goes through the page's mouse and keyboard (a phone keyboard's composed text through `InsertText`).
- **On a touch screen a tap is a click and a drag is a scroll**; a tap on a field the page was told of focuses the text box with that field's keyboard (`inputmode`; `type=password` so the phone's keyboard learns nothing; `autocomplete=one-time-code`) inside the gesture, so one tap raises the keyboard. The page on top — the tab or its newest open popup — is shown and typed into.
- The page is a served string (`HandoffPage`): WebChat's palette, no typed glyphs, full-screen with `interactive-widget=resizes-content`.
- **A passkey cannot be used through it**, by design: a passkey answers only a browser on the site's own origin near the person's device, and the container has no authenticator, USB key or hybrid transport. `DockerCompose/camoufox/server.js` turns off Firefox's USB transport (`security.webauth.webauthn_enable_usbtoken`), which made a passkey ask wait out its timeout behind an unseen prompt: every ask is now refused at once as a cancel and the site offers its other ways, while the API sites feature-detect stays (`LoginHandoffBrowserTests`; rebuild the image after changing it). The view says so in a standing line: our scripts run in an isolated world, and seeing the page's own WebAuthn call would take `main_world_eval` and an override of `navigator.credentials` a login page's bot check can see.

**A dropped view is not an ended login, and the page always has a way on.**
- A phone back from its authenticator app finds a closed socket: the page asks `GET …/handoff/state` (by the view cookie: whether the login is still open, the site, `canRemember`, where it was asked from) and connects again while it is open — on return to the page, on `online`, with backoff; it ends only on 401 or `open: false`.
- The link's GET serves the page again to the browser that claimed it while its login is open, which takes the login up through `state`, so a reload is not "this link has been used".
- The claim and `state` say where the login was asked (`from`: `chat` — the chat client and a voice login's link — `telegram`, or `elsewhere`), and the page words its way back by it. The spent-link page, the logins page and the relay's offline page link to the chat (`/`).
- Connecting again resends the last size unless the width changed, so a keyboard that is up does not shrink the site.
- The chat client remembers the conversation on screen for the window (`OpenTopicEffect`, sessionStorage) and opens it when reloaded there, so coming back lands where the agent's reply arrives.
- A change to the handoff script changes its pinned hash: the last release's stays in `BrowsePagePolicy.ScriptHashes` for one release (`PreviousHandoffScript`).

**Done wakes the conversation that asked.** The browse server is a channel server too (`BufferAlways`, subscriber `channel-websearch`, no outbound surface), dialled as the `websearch` channel endpoint. Done emits one `LoginWake`: the asking context's conversation, agent and user, `ReplyTo` its origin target (a satellite's address included), origin `Login`. Grouping is by conversation and agent, so it queues behind a running turn of a live group, and a group that is not live delivers to the reply target.

**A voice login's link goes to the person's chat client; its answer comes back on the satellite.** nabu cannot speak a link, so `web_login` on a `voice`-origin turn also emits a fire into a new chat-client conversation (`LoginWake.LinkInChatClient`: reply target `signalr` with no conversation, so SignalR mints one; the voice turn's own principal, carrying the account; user and sender the account's user id, since this server learns no display name; origin `Login`) asking the agent to present the link, and WebChat's end-of-stream Web Push pings the space. The tool's answer carries no link, only that it is in WebChat. The chat client opens only behind the account's own sign-in, which is what makes a link safe there, and the topic SignalR mints is owned by the fire's principal's account (`ConversationFactory`). The voice guest has no account and is refused before anything opens. The wake's reply target is the voice turn's own, satellite address included, so a live satellite session speaks the answer and, with none, it is bound as an announcement on that satellite alone.

**Remembered state is sealed, and a save is an allowlist.**
- `RedisPersonStateStore` keeps one record per person under their account's user id (`websearch:person:{userId}`), sealed by Domain's `SealingKey` (`Domain/Security/Sealing/`) under `Logins:StateKey` from secrets (`LOGINS__STATEKEY`, base64 of 32 bytes, read by `SealingKey.Parse`; empty is remembering off, malformed fails startup). The user id is the associated data, so a record copied under another account's key does not open. The layout (AES-GCM, version byte 2) is the one this store wrote before sealing moved into Domain, so those records, and the tenant kit's move's copies, still open.
- A record it cannot open (no key, or the wrong one) throws `PersonStateUnreadableException`, and every `asMe` and `web_login` for that person refuses as `unsupported_operation` (`BrowseStatus.LoginsUnreadable`) rather than browsing logged out; a record of the first, never-deployed format (version 1, bare domains) reads as nothing remembered.
- The record holds Playwright storage state (cookies, localStorage, IndexedDB — all three round-trip on Camoufox) and the remembered logins.
- **A login is a record, not a domain** (`SiteLogin`): its `Site` is the registrable domain of the url handed to `web_login`, and its `Domains` every domain the handoff touched — of its main-frame hosts and of every cookie added or changed between the lease's snapshot and the settle, because an SSO iframe or a cross-domain API sets cookies on hosts the main frame never visits. The snapshot falls back to the record's cookies when the person has no context yet, since the next context is restored from exactly that. A domain is `RegistrableDomain`, by the vendored public suffix list (`Domain/Tools/Web/public_suffix_list.dat`, ICANN and private sections, refreshed by hand), so people's sites on a shared host such as github.io stay apart.
- **Every save writes only what a remembered login covers** (`StorageStateFilter.Only`), so an unticked login's cookies, and a third party's, never reach the disk; a domain a remembered and a live-only login share (a provider both sign in through) is written because the remembered one needs it.
- Done ticked adds or replaces the site's remembered login (domains merged) and drops its live-only one; unticked removes a remembered login of that site, if any, and makes it live-only; neither touches another login, so an unticked login through Google leaves Google remembered for the logins that need it. A handoff that runs out is live-only unless its site is already remembered; one that touched nothing is no login.
- **Live-only logins are held in memory only** (`PersonLogins`), beside the context that holds them, because they die with it (a written list would be stale after one restart); all are forgotten when the contexts close with their connection (`IPersonContexts.ContextsClosed`).
- **A full export happens only when a login is kept or forgotten**; the save after use — `Logins:SaveDebounceSeconds` after the person's first request since the last save, never pushed off by steady traffic, never while a lease is open — exports cookies alone (`IPersonContexts.ExportCookiesAsync`) laid over the stored origins: it exists only so rotated cookies survive a restart, and a mail client's IndexedDB runs to tens of megabytes. A record past `Logins:MaxRecordBytes` is written without IndexedDB, then without localStorage, never without its cookies, with a warning.
- A login is used when a request goes to its own site, not to a provider it shares. No model tool reads or writes the store.

**A person sees and revokes their logins on a page; the model can only hand them it.** `web_logins` mints a link to `/browse/<household>/logins/<token>` under the handoff's rules — one claim, ten minutes, bound to the person's account when minted (`LoginsPages`), so nothing the page sends can name somebody else. It and the handoff's link keep their own link-claimed cookies rather than the accounts session, so somebody who only uses Telegram opens either from their phone. The page lists `PersonLogins.LoginsAsync` by site — remembered or live-only, when handed off and last used, the other domains it covers — with Forget by site. Forget removes that login and strips only the domains no remaining login (remembered or live-only) still covers, from the live context — rebuilt from its state without them (`IPersonContexts.ReplaceStateAsync`, closing their tabs: the only way to clear localStorage without a per-origin API; skipped when nothing is stripped) — and from the record, so forgetting a shop leaves the Google session another login signs in with. It is refused while one of the person's leases is open. `PersonLogins.HasLoginAsync` answers whether a person holds a login whose site or domains cover a given site. No model tool lists or forgets: a page must not be able to talk the agent into revoking a session or covering its tracks.

**Password values never leave a snapshot.** The accessibility snapshot reports a filled password field as `AccessibilitySnapshotService.MaskedPassword` and an empty one as empty, in every context — whoever typed it.
